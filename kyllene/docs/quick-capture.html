Quick capture

A small window you open from anywhere, even when Kyllene is in the background: ask Hermes and read the answer right there, note something down or add a task, with the text you have selected or a screenshot.

Open it

Way How
System-wide shortcut Switch it on in Settings › Quick capture (suggested Ctrl+Alt+Space). The desktop asks you once to confirm it; change the keys in your system settings (Apps › Kyllene › Global shortcuts).
Tray icon Its menu has Quick capture… and Screenshot → Hermes…. On GNOME it needs the AppIndicator extension.
Palette Ctrl+K › Quick capture… or Screenshot → Hermes….

What it does

Pick one of three kinds at the top:

Kind What happens
Ask Hermes A question in a new conversation. The answer appears in the small window; Continue in the main window (Enter) opens the conversation in Kyllene, New question starts again. A question you drop does not clutter your conversation list.
Note Hermes gets "Make a note: …" and stores it its own way.
Task Into a Tasklist of a project you choose (and its section), the Kanban Inbox (a parked card, nothing starts it on its own), or Hermes' own To-do.
  • Selected text: text you selected in another app is attached by itself; From clipboard uses the clipboard instead.
  • Screenshot: the system picker lets you take the whole screen, a window or an area. A tasklist stores only the text, not the picture.
  • Answer in the main window: on = Kyllene comes up with the new conversation; off (default) = the answer shows in the small window and the conversation runs in a background tab.
  • When Hermes needs your approval or an answer, the small window says so: continue in the main window.
  • The window remembers what you picked for the last send (the kind, where a task goes, the project and tasklist, where the answer shows) and offers it again next time. It grows to fit the form.

On Wayland

Wayland gives the selected text of another app only to the window with focus, so Kyllene reads it once the capture window is focused. The small window may fall behind another one after you click elsewhere; keep it on top through its window menu (Alt+Space › Always on Top).
